# Battle music and site audio settings

Status: draft [#170](https://github.com/ewei068/pokestar/pull/170) on `codex/battle-music`, stacked on SFX [#169](https://github.com/ewei068/pokestar/pull/169), 2026-10-18. This document records accepted behavior.

## Accepted behavior

- A new page load or entry into another battle chooses a new random track from the default pool. The chosen track loops for that page visit; battle turns and polling do not reroll it. Track choice is per listener, not stored in the durable battle.
- A specific trainer, raid, dungeon, or individual battle can request a theme. A theme can contain one or more tracks. Default battle music applies when no theme is configured or the web client does not recognize the theme ID.
- The site header has a Settings menu with master mute, Music and Sound effects sliders. These preferences persist locally in the browser. The old `pokestar:battle-sfx` level and mute migrate to `pokestar:audio-settings` on first load; account-level sync is outside this change.
- Real battle pages attempt to start music automatically after the battle and its theme are known. They show no playback button; users can mute or adjust the Music slider in Settings. Rejected startup attempts produce a console warning without an error prompt. Ordinary pointer, touch and keyboard input retries blocked or interrupted playback directly in the event handler. Returning to a visible page, a `pageshow` event, or window focus also attempts recovery. The chosen track is retained during recovery.
- Hiding a real battle tab pauses music; returning to the visible tab automatically attempts to resume the same track from its current position. Pending startup cannot leave music playing after the tab is hidden. Recovery handles the media element and Web Audio context independently. A battle page stops music and removes its recovery listeners when it unmounts. Full-page navigation and shareable battle URLs remain in place; no audio permission is assumed to survive a new document. Development story previews do not autoplay or register automatic recovery handlers and retain their manual Play/Pause control. Battle SFX remain available in production as well as development.

## Data and playback boundaries

- `src/config/npcConfig.js` gives Blue, Red, Gold, Steven, Cynthia, Palmer, each raid, and every active dungeon a `musicThemeId`. Other trainers leave it unset. `src/battle/service/battleMusic.js` resolves an explicit `record.mode.musicThemeId` override first, then the relevant encounter config. `battleWebView.js` projects only `presentation.musicThemeId`; it does not expose raw mode details. This is the same display-only principle used by sprite URLs in `/api/catalog`.
- `web/src/battles/audio/musicCatalog.json` maps theme IDs to the static files in `web/public/music/`. An unknown ID uses the default pool. Once a real battle has loaded, missing theme metadata also falls back to the default pool for compatibility with older battle responses.
- `useBattleMusic.ts` chooses a track when the battle and theme are known. It streams an `HTMLAudioElement` through a Web Audio gain node. The Music slider is multiplied by an optional per-track `volume` scalar; omitted tracks use the catalog `defaultVolume` of 0.6, about 40% below the slider. The slider stays independent of SFX and does not rely on the media element's `volume` property, which iOS Safari may ignore ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AudioVideo/Conceptual/Using_HTML5_Audio_Video/Device-SpecificConsiderations/Device-SpecificConsiderations.html)). `useBattleAudio.ts` continues to schedule short SFX from reached playback frames with its separate gain.
- The Settings menu is in the site shell. Battle playback remains inside `BattlePage`; it does not alter combat rules, events, or the battle session transport.

## Verification and remaining review

Backend theme and projection tests, browser track-selection and preference-migration tests, the full unit suites, and the production web build passed locally on 2026-10-18. Browser preview confirmed Play/Pause, persisted volume after reload, and settings layout at a narrow phone viewport. These checks establish wiring, not listening quality or iOS audio behavior.

The later mobile recovery change passed seven hook integration tests with simulated media and audio contexts, plus the production build. These cover touch retry after rejection, pause on hiding and resume on return, pending startup finishing while hidden, resuming an interrupted context while media is already playing, recovery on page return/focus, manual-only stories, stale rejection races, and cleanup. They do not establish actual iPhone or Android behavior; check on a real device after reload, switching apps, and returning to the battle.

Next, listen to complete tracks and loop boundaries with real battle SFX on desktop and a real phone. Check whether autoplay is allowed in each target browser, whether the default 25% music gain leaves attacks legible, whether the default, dungeon, trainer, and raid tracks fit their encounters, whether music and SFX coexist on iOS, and whether Safari honors the streamed media-to-Web-Audio gain path. Adjust track choices or mix from that evidence. If exact gapless looping matters, edit loop sources or use a different loop strategy after that review.
